# Demo film: script (draft for Maria's OK)

Narrator: Edge "Andrew". About 2:55. Words in **bold** are lit on screen as callouts.
Spoken text only, apart from the on-screen notes in italics.

---

## 1 · The problem (0:00–0:25)

*Illustrations in Paper & Ink: a phone with a fake "verify your wallet" pop-up; a laptop with a bug
crawling across it; a sticky note with twelve seed words. Each ends with a wallet balance draining
to zero in carmine. Then the ledger: rows of transactions scrolling forever, all public.
On screen: "$3.4 billion stolen from crypto in 2025" · Source: Chainalysis, Dec 2025, via The Block.*

> Almost every crypto wallet is guarded by one thing: a private key.
> A fake website asks for it. Malware copies it. A backup ends up on a sticky note.
> Whoever holds the key owns the money, and a blockchain can't undo a theft.
> Last year alone, more than three billion dollars was stolen.

## 2 · Q-Day (0:25–0:40)

*A quantum chip glowing violet; public data on the ledger turning into red keys, one after another.
Poster card: Q-DAY.*

> And there's a bigger threat coming. One day, quantum computers will work out private keys from
> data that is already public. That day has a name: Q-Day. On Q-Day, every ordinary key is as good
> as stolen, all at once.

## 3 · The dare (0:40–0:55)

*Site: the 0–100 loader, "HERE'S THE KEY. GOOD LUCK.", zoom on the key card, Copy the key,
the live balance. Callouts: **published on purpose** · **live from the chain**.*

> So we skipped the wait. Steal This Vault publishes the real private key of a crypto vault.
> Copy it. Use it. The money is still inside: about ten QOR, read live from the chain.

## 4 · Two locks (0:55–1:12)

*Site: the two-locks scene plays: KEY FITS, then REFUSED.
Callouts: **lock 1: the ordinary key** · **lock 2: quantum-safe, ML-DSA-87** · **checked by the chain, and again by the vault**.*

> Because here, one key was never enough. The stolen key opens the first lock. The second lock
> needs a quantum-safe signature from a key that never leaves the owner. The chain checks it,
> and then the vault checks it again. No second signature, no money.

## 5 · Replay (1:12–1:24)

*Site: "Now attacking", REFUSED stamps landing. Callout: **real attempts, from the vault's own record**.*

> These aren't animations of what might happen. They are real break-in attempts, replayed from the
> record the vault keeps on the chain. Every one hits the same wall.

## 6 · Scoreboard (1:24–1:42)

*Site: "SAME KEY. TWO ENDINGS.", the twin ROBBED next to the vault STILL FULL, zoom on 39.996 QOR.
Callouts: **the one-lock twin** · **counted by the vault itself**.*

> To prove the second lock is what matters, we built a twin: the same vault with only the ordinary
> lock, like almost every wallet today. Same key. The twin was robbed. And the vault keeps its own
> count of what one lock would have lost: nearly forty QOR. It lost nothing.

## 7 · Attack lab (1:42–2:02)

*Site: Run all 19, stamps ticking down, "18 OF 18 REFUSED · 0 QOR LOST", then the twin's comparison.
Callouts: **anyone can try** · **18 real methods** · **live, nothing recorded here**.*

> And you don't have to trust us. Anyone can open the attack lab and try eighteen real ways to use
> a stolen key, live. Eighteen out of eighteen: refused. Zero lost. Run the same robbery on the
> twin, and it pays.

## 8 · The Q-Day drill (2:02–2:20)

*Site: the SURVIVED Q-DAY banner (0 · 0 · Yes), the Today / Q-Day switch flipping to red, the proof card.
Callouts: **24 hours with the thief** · **result read from the chain**.*

> On the eighteenth of November, the fest staged Q-Day for real. A bot held our key for twenty-four
> hours and tried everything. Zero thefts. Zero lost. And the owner, holding both keys, still got in.

## 9 · Alerts and rescue (2:20–2:34)

*Site: the alert phone card, then the three rescue steps. Callouts: **warned while the money is safe** · **only the owner can do this**.*

> When someone tries, the owner is told straight away, while the money is still there. And if it
> ever comes to it, there's a way out: shut the side door and move everything to a fresh account.
> Only the owner can.

## 10 · Intruders (2:34–2:44)

*Site: the intruder story, the tries / refused / taken tiles, the money trail. Callout: **every thief leaves a trail**.*

> And every thief leaves a trail: who tried, what they got, and where the money went next.

## 11 · Close (2:44–2:55)

*Poster card: KEYS WILL LEAK. ADD A SECOND LOCK. Then the logo, Steal This Vault,
"The key is free. The money isn't."*

> Keys will leak. Q-Day will come. The answer is a second, quantum-safe lock, built so any app can
> use it. Steal This Vault. The key is free. The money isn't.
